# Step 3.10: Report complementary switching metrics

Status: complete; independently verified ([review](../reviews/step-3.10-independent-review.md)).
November 16, 2026.

## Why

Steps 3.1–3.9 produced several "switching" numbers. They count different
things, so they cannot be compared directly. This step puts them side by side,
each with its population, denominator and order dependence. It also checks how
well the training surrogate `2p(1−p)`, the term the power-aware loss will use in
4.x, predicts measured switching.

## What this shows

1. **The surrogate is a good proxy for settled logical switching here.**
   `2p(1−p)` is within 1% of the measured rate in every layer. Overall the
   gap is 0.00320. The finite-sample factor `n/(n−1)` accounts for 0.00077
   (24%). The other 0.00243 (76%) comes from this particular order, and it is
   within shuffle noise (point 2).
2. **The saved test order behaves like a random order.** Its rate is 0.3498.
   The random-order expectation is 0.3473, and 200 shuffles give a standard
   deviation of 0.0033, so the difference is about 0.7–0.8 standard
   deviations in every layer. Points 1 and 2 are not independent evidence:
   with a random-looking order, `2p(1−p)` matches each gate almost by
   construction. A correlated stream (video frames, sorted classes) would
   break this, and the surrogate would then misjudge switching.
3. **Switching grows with depth.** It rises from 0.28 in layer 1 to 0.42 in
   layer 4. 499 gates (12.5%) never switch on these inputs, almost all in
   layers 1–3. The cause is not analysed here.
4. **The surrogate misses glitches.** On the routed core nets of 3.9, every
   settled change comes with about 1.7 extra timed transitions. Those nets
   cover the learned gates and also the popcount, score and argmax logic, so
   they are a wider population than the surrogate's 4,000 gate outputs. A
   settled-only count of those nets misses roughly 63% of their timed
   transitions on this 8-image workload. Whether lowering `2p(1−p)` also
   lowers total switching, glitches included, has to be tested in 4.x.

## How

`scripts/report_switching_metrics.py` loads `runs/digits_w1000/model.json` and
the saved inputs. It recomputes each layer's outputs with `dlgn/hard.py` and
computes the surrogate, the saved-order rate, the exact random-order
expectation and a 200-shuffle check. It then reads the tracked 3.6–3.9 results.
It stops if:

- the per-layer and input counts differ from the RTL values recorded in 3.7
- `hard.toggle_rate` disagrees
- the shuffle mean departs from the exact expectation

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/report_switching_metrics.py
```

Output: [step-3.10-switching-metrics.json](../results/step-3.10-switching-metrics.json),
which records hashes of the model, the inputs and every tracked result it reads.
Tests: `tests/test_switching_metrics.py` covers the brute-force permutation
expectation, constant gates, reproduction of the 3.7 counts and tamper rejection.

## Example

A gate that outputs 1 on 225 of 450 images has p = 0.5, so `2p(1−p) = 0.5`.
Shuffle the images, and two neighbours differ with probability
`2·225·225/(450·449) = 0.5011`. A gate stuck at 1 has p = 1, so its surrogate
is 0 and it never switches.

## Limits

- These are different populations, windows and workloads. Don't add,
  subtract or rank them as if they measured the same thing.
- The surrogate here uses hard outputs on the test set, whereas training uses
  soft outputs on training batches.
- The model is the historical exploratory baseline (its test set was already
  inspected), not the matched control that 4.x will train.
- The glitch ratio comes from 8 images on the routed UART board.
- None of these metrics is power or energy.
