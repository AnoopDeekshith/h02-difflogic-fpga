# Step 4.5: Repeat the selected penalty across seeds

Status: complete; independently verified ([review](../reviews/step-4.5-independent-review.md)).
Recorded November 18, 2026.

## What happened

We held **lambda 0.3** fixed and trained four new matched control/penalty pairs.
Settled logical switching fell in **all four new pairs**, averaging **16.71
percent lower** than each pair's control. However, only **one of the four**
stayed within the frozen one-percentage-point accuracy allowance.

The lower-switching pattern repeated in these runs, but acceptable accuracy
did not. Lambda 0.3 is therefore **not a consistently acceptable optimization
or a robust default under this rule**. This is useful evidence about the
tradeoff; it is not an execution failure to hide or a reason to relax the rule.

## Why repeat model seeds

[Step 4.4](4.4-sweep-penalty-strengths.md) selected lambda 0.3 using model seed
zero. One initialization can give an attractive result by chance. We need to
check the selected setting on additional initializations rather than assume
that the first result is typical.

A **model seed** controls initial connections, initial gate-choice weights and
minibatch shuffle randomness. Changing it tests variability in the training
procedure. Within a pair, the control and penalty use the same seed, so their
starting conditions match. The **split seed** remains zero throughout: the
training/validation partitions and validation image order do not change.

We trained **eight new models**, lambda zero and lambda 0.3 for each of seeds
1, 2, 3 and 4. The seed-zero control from step 4.3 and seed-zero lambda-0.3
model from step 4.4 were explicitly reused as the discovery pair. Together
there are ten models and five pairs, but only four pairs are new repeats.

## The rule was fixed before the repeats

The saved plan froze lambda 0.3 after the seed-zero sweep. We did not search
for a different lambda on each new seed or remove disappointing pairs.
The predeclared conservative consistency rule required **all four new pairs**
to satisfy both conditions:

1. Strictly fewer settled learned-gate changes than their own matched control.
2. At most one percentage point of hard validation accuracy loss.

The accuracy tolerance is the existing working experimental policy, not a
professor requirement. We kept it unchanged. The primary summary excludes
seed zero because it helped choose lambda. A secondary summary includes it
but is clearly labeled **selection-informed**.

Each model used digits, four layers of 1,000 gates, tau 10, Adam learning rate
0.01, batch size 100 and the final checkpoint after 60 epochs. CPU execution
used OMP/MKL thread limits of one. All runs used 1,077 training and 270
validation images; evaluation of the 450 test images remained deferred.
No earlier favorable epoch was substituted for the final epoch.

Both correct-prediction counts below are out of the same 270 validation images.
Switching reduction is relative to that row's control, not the historical
`runs/digits_w1000` baseline.

|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | Reused discovery | 249 | 247 | 0.7407 | 17.1731 percent | Yes |
| 1 | New repeat | 248 | 245 | 1.1111 | 16.0357 percent | No |
| 2 | New repeat | 253 | 247 | 2.2222 | 16.7279 percent | No |
| 3 | New repeat | 248 | 248 | 0 | 16.2527 percent | Yes |
| 4 | New repeat | 249 | 242 | 2.5926 | 17.8436 percent | No |

For example, seed 1 loses three correct images: `3 / 270 × 100 = 1.1111
percentage points`. That narrowly exceeds the one-point budget and must still
fail. Seed 3 has the same total number correct and lower switching, but
reporting only that seed would conceal the other three failed accuracy checks.
Equal total accuracy also does not imply that both models made mistakes on
exactly the same images.

## Understand the averages and their limits

For the **four new seeds only**, the mean within-pair relative logical
reduction is **16.71498 percent**, with **sample standard deviation 0.80604
percentage points of reduction**. Mean accuracy loss is **1.48148 percentage
points**, with sample standard deviation **1.17121 percentage points**.
Switching improves in 4/4 pairs; the accuracy budget and joint rule pass in
only 1/4. The conservative all-pairs condition therefore fails.

The standard deviation describes how these model-seed results vary around
their average. It is not a confidence interval, a significance test or
uncertainty across independent datasets. All models reuse **the same 270
validation images**; five pairs do not create 1,350 unique validation examples.

The mean reduction gives each pair's percentage equal weight. A different
summary pools raw counts first: the four controls have 1,513,219 changes and
the penalties have 1,260,269, a **16.71602 percent pooled reduction**. These
numbers are close but are different calculations and remain separately labeled.
Each model has `4000 gates × 269 image pairs = 1,076,000` change opportunities.

The **secondary five-pair summary**, including discovery, gives mean relative
reduction **16.80661 percent**, sample standard deviation **0.72750**, and
mean accuracy loss **1.33333 percentage points**, sample standard deviation
**1.06703**. Two of five pairs pass. Because seed zero helped select lambda,
this secondary summary does not replace the primary four-repeat assessment.

## Evidence and reproduction

Primary evidence is `runs/step4.5-seeds.vyq2h2nf/`. The prewritten `plan.json`
records the fixed strength, seeds, eight new training commands, reused-pair
fingerprints, consistency rule and aggregation definitions. `source/` preserves
eight Python source files and `selection_policy.json` preserves the policy.

New `seed_*_control/` and `seed_*_penalty/` directories contain models,
checkpoints, validation data/order, reference outputs, metrics, protocols,
manifests and statuses. Separate training logs and per-seed selection reports
retain every outcome. `aggregate.json` and `summarize.py` document the totals
and calculations. The [tracked summary](../results/step-4.5-repeated-seeds.json)
preserves results and fingerprints; raw gitignored runs still need separate
archival. Historical-baseline and reused seed-zero artifacts were preserved.

To reproduce training, inspect the plan's eight commands and use **new output
directories** for all eight models, preserving the source and policy first.
Keep the OMP/MKL limits and matched settings. Do not overwrite primary evidence
or `runs/digits_w1000`. The plan also records each pair's selection command;
recheck those with new selection-output filenames. These are reproduction
instructions, not additional training performed for this walkthrough.

The [independent review](../reviews/step-4.5-independent-review.md) records the
artifact and aggregation checks. Both audits can be rerun without training or

## Verification and next research decision

Independent scalar evaluation matched **2,700 image computations** across all
ten models, including integer scores, lowest-index class choices and exact
gate-change totals. The audit checked final epoch 60, 660 Adam updates per
parameter, checkpoints, reconstructed matched initialization/shuffle behavior,
RNG states, split/order identities, source/policy archives and fingerprints.
A separate summary check reproduced means, sample deviations, ranges, pass
counts and pooling. Final documentation review passed with no blocking finding.

No executable training code changed. The latest 285-test regression result
belongs to step 4.10, not a fresh suite run here. No new test performance,
RTL equivalence, hardware activity, glitches, resources, timing, power, energy
or novelty evidence is supplied. Unweighted logical changes omit electrical
loads and clock activity. The previously inspected digits test remains
exploratory, and four repeats do not establish a general-population benefit.

The next research decision is whether to evaluate a **weaker fixed lambda**
under a newly recorded plan before promoting a robust default. We should not
cherry-pick seed 3 or loosen the accuracy tolerance after seeing these results.
Step 4.6's own RTL checks remain pending and are separate from deciding whether
the training tradeoff is consistently acceptable.
