# Step 4.15: Test a layer-targeted switching penalty

Status: complete; independently verified ([pre-run review](../reviews/step-4.15-prerun-review.md),
[results review](../reviews/step-4.15-independent-review.md)). Recorded November 19, 2026.
Summary: [step-4.15-layer-targeting.json](../results/step-4.15-layer-targeting.json).
Plan: [layer-targeting-v1.json](../experiments/layer-targeting-v1.json).
Discussion: [layer-targeted penalty](../discussions/2026-11-18-layer-targeted-penalty.md).

## What happened

[Step 4.13](4.13-run-the-screening-probe.md) showed that the uniform penalty's
16% logical saving shrinks to about 3% on mapped iCE40 cells. Most chip
switching sits in the popcount, which layer 4 feeds. We tested whether pushing
the penalty toward particular layers carries over to the chip better.

**It did not.** The frozen outcome is **TARGETING_NOT_SHOWN**.

## What we compared

The penalty is `λ × Σ w_l · mean_l(2p(1−p)) / Σ w_l`. The weights decide where
the push goes; normalization keeps the total size equal.

| Profile | Weights on layers 1–4 | Push per gate vs uniform |
|---|---|---|
| U (uniform) | equal | 1× everywhere |
| L4 | 0, 0, 0, 1 | 4× on layer 4 |
| L34 | 0, 0, 1, 1 | 2× on layers 3–4 |
| M23 | 0, 1, 1, 0 | 2× on layers 2–3 |
| RAMP | 1, 2, 3, 4 | 0.4× → 1.6× |

Each profile ran at λ = 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3, the same grid for all. Seed 1
chose the best targeted (T*) and best uniform (U*) model: lowest mapped core
toggles (M1) within 1 accuracy point. Seeds 2–4 then confirmed. "Better"
required every confirmation seed to be within 1 point, at least 2% lower on
the chip, and lower than the comparator.

## Results

**Discovery, seed 1** (control 248/270 correct). Selected: T* = RAMP 0.3
(2.41% fewer chip toggles) and U* = uniform 0.2 (2.64%). Last-layer-only (L4)
changed logical switching by only 0.4–0.7% and chip toggles by 0.4–1.0%.

**Confirmation, seeds 2–4, RAMP 0.3 vs uniform 0.2:**

| Seed | RAMP drop | RAMP chip reduction | Uniform 0.2 chip reduction | Pass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 1.48 pts | 2.32% | 2.11% (itself 1.48 pts drop) | no: accuracy |
| 3 | 0.37 pts | 3.49% | 2.25% | yes |
| 4 | 2.59 pts | 2.29% | 3.03% | no: accuracy and lower |

The means (2.70% vs 2.46%) must not be read as "targeting is slightly better".
They mix ineligible models, the gap comes mostly from seed 3, and RAMP cost
more accuracy (1.48 vs 0.62 points mean drop).

## What we learned

- Across all 28 models, chip-level core toggles fell by at most about 3.6%
  within the 1-point accuracy rule.
- For uniform, RAMP, M23 and L34, only about one fifth of the logical saving
  survives mapping.
- **Why layer 4 resists is unknown.** Even with 4× per-gate weight, layer-4
  logical changes fell only 0.7–1.5%. The gates did not become constant. An
  early explanation, that their switching "is the vote", was checked by the
  verifier and is **not supported**: a perfectly selective voter would flip
  less than these gates do.

## How

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_layer_targeting.py discover --policy docs/experiments/layer-targeting-v1.json
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_layer_targeting.py confirm runs/step4.15-targeting.v7r6ms06
```

Code: optional `layer_weights` in `dlgn/model.py` `activity_penalty`;
`--act-layer-weights` in `scripts/train.py`; runner
`scripts/run_layer_targeting.py`, which reuses the 4.13 measurement unchanged.
Gates: retrained controls and uniform λ = 0.3 models are byte-identical to
step 4.5 (8/8), and seed-1 chip counts reproduce 4.13 exactly. The verifier
recomputed all 28 models with its own NumPy code and reran three mapped
measurements with identical results.

## Limitations

- Zero-delay mapped standalone core: no routing, glitches, power or energy.
- Digits 8×8, one network size, one chip family. Three confirmation seeds.
- Seed-1 discovery was informed by the 4.13 probe.
- Test data stay unused.
