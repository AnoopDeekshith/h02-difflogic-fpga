# Step 1.11: Run the regression tests

Status: completed. Notes recorded November 13, 2026.

## Why we needed this

Repeatable tests help catch mistakes when we change the exporter or interface.

## What we did

We ran the core/gate tests and UART regressions. Step 2.7 also adds constraint and synchronizer checks.

## How it works

The original tests cover the truth tables, soft formulas and random-network RTL for both register configurations. UART tests exercise real serial pins, reference scores, packet recovery, input padding and tied scores. Constraint tests use a small synthesized wrapper and deliberately corrupt its constraints or wiring.

## Small example

A test intentionally bypasses the second RX synchronizer register; the constraint checker must reject that unsafe connection.

## Result and its limits

Step 2.7 had 14 passing tests. Step 2.8 brings the suite to **45 passing tests**:
14 earlier cases, 16 independently written exhaustive head-pipeline cases,
11 artifact-validation cases and 4 data-partition cases. The exhaustive cases
check 16,384 synthetic inputs. Simulator-dependent tests require the relevant
tool; a skipped check is not a hardware verification result. See
[PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md) for the latest result and
[the independent review](../reviews/step-2.8-independent-review.md) for its scope.

Step 2.9 adds 18 independently written archived-timing report cases, bringing
the suite at that step to **63 passing tests**. These check changed hashes, stale
parameters, failed attempts, invalid clock/path evidence and older records
missing tool identity. See [the timing walkthrough](2.9-read-and-verify-clock-timing.md)
and its [independent review](../reviews/step-2.9-independent-review.md).

Step 2.10 adds 8 independent performance cases, bringing the suite at that step to
**71 passing tests** (15.14 seconds, no skips). They check all four register
configurations, input bubbles, full UART stop-bit boundaries, the shipped
performance bench and model/workload matching. See
[step 2.10](2.10-compare-latency-and-throughput.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-2.10-independent-review.md).

Step 3.2 adds 14 independent waveform-recording cases, bringing the suite at that
step to **85 passing tests** (November 16: 9.34 seconds, no skips). Four cases
exercise recording on/off across all layer/head register combinations; ten
check valid aliases/unknown initialization and reject recording faults. See
[step 3.2](3.2-verify-waveform-recording.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.2-independent-review.md).

Step 3.3 adds 10 independent full-capture cases, bringing the suite at that step to
**95 passing tests** (November 16: 10.83 seconds, no skips). They check complete
recorded transactions for all four register configurations, repeated zero
inputs and tied scores, and corrupted capture ledgers. See
[step 3.3](3.3-capture-the-baseline-waveform.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.3-independent-review.md).

Step 3.4 adds 35 independent activity-counter cases, bringing the suite at that step
to **130 passing tests** (November 16: 10.16 seconds, no skips). They exercise
bit-level four-state/alias counting, time boundaries, format rejection and
capture/analysis provenance guards. See
[step 3.4](3.4-count-waveform-transitions.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.4-independent-review.md).

Step 3.5 adds 22 independent measurement-window cases, bringing that step's
suite to **152 passing tests** (November 16: 11.78 seconds, no skips). They check
all four register configurations, repeated inputs, endpoint alignment and
semantic/hash faults, including between-sample and drain input pulses. See
[step 3.5](3.5-define-activity-windows.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.5-independent-review.md).

Step 3.6 adds 20 independent category/activity cases, bringing that step's
suite to **172 passing tests** (November 16: 12.25 seconds, no skips). These
cover all four register configurations, separate raw/settled oracles, aliases,
repeated inputs, intermediate known/unknown pulses and preserved rejection
reports. See [step 3.6](3.6-separate-activity-categories.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.6-independent-review.md).

Step 3.7 adds 22 independent NumPy/RTL activity cases, bringing the current
suite to **194 passing tests** (November 16: 13.83 seconds, no skips). They
check four register configurations against separate scalar/VCD oracles, bit
positions, exact states despite equal toggle totals, copies/registers, explicit
ties, repeated inputs, intermediate known/X events and reference/provenance
faults. An initial test incorrectly assumed repeated inputs implied tied scores;
the zero-change and deliberate tie fixtures now check those properties
separately. See [step 3.7](3.7-cross-check-numpy-and-rtl-activity.md) and its
[review](../reviews/step-3.7-independent-review.md).
