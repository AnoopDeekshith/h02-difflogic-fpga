## Why

Step 4.5 showed that lambda 0.3 cuts settled logical switching by about 17%
but costs about 1.5 percentage points of accuracy. Whether that is worth it
depends on energy, which we cannot yet measure (no supported power tool; see
the [pilot plan discussion](../discussions/2026-11-18-small-saving-publication-and-pilot-plan.md)).

Before spending days on hardware measurements, we decide **in writing** what
result would make us stop. If we chose the line after seeing the numbers, we
could always argue that whatever came out is "worth it". That is the same
moving-goalposts risk the frozen accuracy allowance protects against.

## What the rule says

**Threshold: 2%**, Anoop's judgement of the smallest energy saving worth
pursuing.

**Why a core switching number can screen energy.** Total power is core logic
plus clock, interface, I/O and static power. The penalty only changes the core.

> Example: the core's switching falls 1.5%. Even if all core energy scaled
> with switching, total energy falls by no more than 1.5%. That is below 2%, so
> we can stop without a power tool.

The bound works only one way. A core reduction of 2% or more does not mean
2% less energy; it only justifies paying for a real power estimate. It also
assumes that each transition in the penalized model costs no less energy
than in the control, and that the rest of the design is unchanged, so resource counts are always
reported, since the penalized model may synthesize to a different size.

**Two metrics, core nets only:**

- **M1 raw:** known 0/1 transitions on core nets.
- **M2 fanout-weighted:** each transition multiplied by how many cell inputs
  the net drives. Example: a net with 100 toggles driving 2 inputs scores 200;
  one with 10 toggles driving 30 inputs scores 300. M2 is a rough capacitance
  stand-in, not energy.

Reduction for a pair: `R = 1 - penalty / control`.

**Two stages:**

| Stage | Data | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 4.13 probe | Seed 1 only, zero-delay mapped netlist, 270 validation images | **PAUSE** if R < 2% on both M1 and M2; otherwise **CONTINUE** |
| 4.14 final | Seeds 1–4 (seed 0 separate), routed with real delays, 3 placement seeds each | **STOP**, **PROCEED** or **INCONCLUSIVE** (below) |

Final decision, checked in this order:

0. **INCONCLUSIVE (implementation failure)** if any primary model fails the
   bit-exact check, fit, routing or 12 MHz timing on any placement seed.
1. **STOP** if mean R < 2% on both metrics. Drop the energy-saving framing;
   use the characterization framing.
2. **PROCEED** if, for at least one metric, mean R ≥ 2% **and** the
   control-minus-penalty difference exceeds twice the placement noise.
3. **INCONCLUSIVE** otherwise.

**Placement noise.** nextpnr places cells using a random seed. Different
placements route wires differently and change glitches. If two placements of
the same model differ by 3%, a 2% model difference means nothing. Noise is the
max-minus-min spread across placement seeds.

**Fixed for every model:** the 270 validation images in saved order, the
head-only core, iCE40UP5K, 12 MHz and unchanged constraints. Before any
activity number counts, each model must match NumPy on all 270 images in RTL
and in zero-delay simulation of its mapped and routed netlists, and on every
image a timed simulation covers. Test data stay
unused.

**Accuracy is unchanged.** Lambda 0.3 enters hardware as an accuracy-failed
diagnostic. Its step 4.5 verdicts under the one-point allowance stay failed.

**What the 2% is a share of.** The threshold applies to the **core's**
switching, as in a streaming accelerator that does useful work every clock.
It is not a board percentage. On the UART board the core is idle most of the
time: in step 3.9, core nets made only about 0.9% of all board transitions
(32,580 of 3.49 million). Under that workload a 17% core reduction is
roughly 0.15% of board transitions (a transition share, not energy).
Board totals are reported separately.

**If something fails.** A model that fails the bit-exact check, fit,
routing or the 12 MHz timing check makes the final outcome INCONCLUSIVE
(implementation failure), never STOP or PROCEED. Constraints are never relaxed.

**After a PAUSE.** Resuming the larger steps needs a new, dated policy, so the
2% line cannot be reinterpreted later.

## The amendment (v1 to v2)

The independent review found three blocking gaps before any measurement:
the stage-aligned window and one-image-per-clock workload cannot be applied
to mapped or routed netlists (B1); there was no outcome after an
implementation failure (B2); and the 270/270 check contradicted the timed
image subset (B3). v2 fixes these with fixed-schedule windows, an
implementation-failure outcome and a split functional gate. It also lists
all ten model folders with hashes, freezes the routed core-net rule, fixes
the timed image count by a formula (1,800 s budget), records placement
hashes and states the scope of the 2%. The threshold, metrics, seeds and
decision structure did not change. v1 stays in git as committed.

## The second amendment (v2 to v3)

The re-review found that v2 would count routed nets that step 3.9 left
'unclassified' (reset chain, TX pin, VCC, interface carries: about 529k
settled changes, identical in both models) as core. That would dilute R about
17-fold toward STOP. v3 uses exactly the nets classified 'core' and reports
all others separately, with an `out_valid_*` sensitivity check. This only
affects the routed stage (4.14) and was fixed before any run.

## How to check it was frozen first

- All policy hashes are listed above; each version was committed before the
  next was written, and v3 before any probe run. The probe script pins v3's
  hash and rejects v1, v2 or an edited v3.
- Before freezing, a search of every step 4.3–4.5 and review run folder found
  no exported RTL, hex vectors, VCD, synthesis or bitstream file.

## Limitations

- Every metric is a switching proxy, not power or energy.
- The 2% threshold is a working judgement, not a professor requirement.
- The "core bound" assumes non-core power is unchanged between models.
- PROCEED does not demonstrate an energy saving; STOP does not erase the
  verified logical-switching result.
